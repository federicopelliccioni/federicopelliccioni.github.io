Research

My PhD investigates how climate warming affects key stages of a bird's life, from migration to reproduction.

My interests bridge morphology, phenology and behaviour, with a focus on how these traits respond to environmental change. I work on two model species, the Rock Sparrow Petronia petronia and the Lesser Kestrel Falco naumanni, combining long-term historical data with ongoing fieldwork.

Phenology
Shifts in the timing and length of the breeding season, and what they mean for reproductive success.
Morphology
Body measurements as a record of how individuals and populations respond to a changing environment.
Behaviour
Mating strategies, colony dynamics and other behavioural traits under climate change.

Study species

Rock Sparrow

Petronia petronia Passera lagia

Italian Alps, province of Turin

I study a Rock Sparrow population breeding in the Italian Alps, already known for mating tactics that differ from those found across the rest of the species' European range. My work explores how the lengthening of the breeding season driven by climate warming has altered survival, mating strategies and other behavioural traits.

Identifying rare mating behaviours with machine learning

We're implementing an automated screening pipeline based on YOLO 26m to screen camera trap videos for rare mating behaviours. In collaboration with Giacomo Bovo Luisetto.

In preparation

Climate Warming effect on Sexual Selection

We're applying various sexual selection metrics on 23 years of reproductive data to quantify climate change impact on sexual selection.Recently presented at ISBE 2026.

Lesser Kestrel

Falco naumanni Grillaio

The Lesser Kestrel is my model for studying phenology, colony dynamics and climate-driven shifts in reproductive success. My work integrates historical monitoring data with behavioural observations.
